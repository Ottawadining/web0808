Worth the drive: three restaurants outside Ottawa

A French-leaning tasting menu in Carleton Place, a bakery-bistro in Smiths Falls and a slice of Spain in Prescott. All within about an hour of the city.

the short version
  • Black Tartan Kitchen, Carleton Place: seasonal tasting menus from chef Ian Carswell, about 40 minutes west.
  • C'est Tout Bakery & Bistro, Smiths Falls: breads, pastries and European-style comfort food, about an hour southwest.
  • Little Barcelona, Prescott: tapas, paella and a Spanish food shop, about an hour south.

Ottawa's best restaurants get plenty of attention. The ones a short drive outside the city get far less, which is a shame, because a few of them are as good as anything downtown, and the drive is half the fun.

Here are three worth putting gas in the car for. Each is about an hour or less from downtown, and each is a different kind of meal.

Black Tartan Kitchen

Carleton Place · about 40 minutes west

Chef-owner Ian Carswell's restaurant on Bridge Street is the most polished of the three. The cooking is rooted in French technique, shaped by years Carswell spent abroad, including time at a vineyard in the Loire Valley and in a top-ranked kitchen in Helsinki, and it's built around what Lanark County and the Ottawa Valley are producing that month.

Dinner is a tasting menu, with four, five or seven courses depending on the night, and the menu changes monthly. There's an à la carte lunch later in the week too. Carswell has competed at the Canadian Culinary Championships, and he took silver at this year's Kitchen Party Ottawa all-star invitational.

Go for: a special-occasion dinner that doesn't require a downtown reservation scramble.

C'est Tout Bakery & Bistro

Smiths Falls · about an hour southwest

The most relaxed of the three, and the one to plan a morning around. C'est Tout is a woman-owned, chef-driven bakery and bistro on Beckwith Street North, cooking French and European-inspired comfort food with local ingredients.

The bakery case is the draw: breads like walnut cranberry, pastries, cinnamon rolls and cheesecake. The bistro side covers breakfast, lunch and a Saturday brunch, with lobster rolls on Fridays in season. It's a daytime spot, so time your drive accordingly.

Go for: a weekend brunch, then a walk along the Rideau Canal, which runs right through town.

Little Barcelona

Prescott · about an hour south

The most surprising of the three. Tucked into an industrial strip on the edge of Prescott, Little Barcelona serves tapas, pinchos and paella that would hold their own in Spain, alongside a shop stocked with Spanish products: jamón ibérico de bellota, cheeses, olives, sea salts and olive oils.

Its owner came to Canada from Barcelona, and the restaurant sources directly from Spanish producers. It also holds the Spanish government's "Restaurants from Spain" certification, given to places that represent the country's cooking authentically.

Go for: a long lunch of tapas and paella, and leave room in the trunk for the shop.

Before you go

All three are small, independent places with limited hours that can change with the seasons. Check before you set out, and book ahead where you can, especially at Black Tartan and Little Barcelona.
